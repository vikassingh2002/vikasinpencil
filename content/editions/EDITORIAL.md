# Editorial model for Agentic AI

## Sources consulted

- [The Economist: World in Brief developer documentation](https://developer.economist.com/docs/v1/the-world-in-brief) distinguishes short news paragraphs from longer bite-sized pieces. This informed the vertical seven-update digest and its six separate short-read panels.
- [The Economist Group: product and editorial formats](https://www.economistgroup.com/businesses/the-economist) describes news summaries, opinion, briefings and special reports as different formats. This informed the mix of four shorter analyses and eight longer explainers/briefings.
- [Economist Education: Writing with Style](https://education.economist.com/blog/what-to-read/writing-with-style) emphasises clear, informative, persuasive prose and making readers' time worthwhile. Agentic AI uses original writing, clear mechanisms, concrete examples and explicit distinctions between evidence and interpretation.
- [Nieman Lab: the app's weekday/weekend redesign](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/06/the-economist-redesigns-its-app-to-promote-short-stories-on-weekdays-and-longer-reads-on-weekends/) reports a distinction between short weekday reading and more immersive longer reading. This informed the navigation between Brief and Weekly.

## Rules for future issues

The issue date is a publication grouping and a knowledge cutoff, not a requirement that every source was published in the preceding week. A conceptual feature may draw on foundational research from years earlier. Keep post-cutoff developments out of retrospective issues. News digest items should still be dated within the stated news window.

Begin with a concrete question or event, state the explanatory argument, show the mechanism, examine trade-offs and counterarguments, and end with a consequence that follows from the evidence. Length must add explanation, examples or useful distinctions. A longer read-time label is not a longer article.

Distinguish company announcements from independently demonstrated outcomes, and research findings from universal claims. Do not invent interviews, scenes presented as reporting, financial results or experiments. Illustrative examples must be recognisable as such. Sources appear with each full article; image sources and licences appear under images and in the credit manifest.

AI in Brief is an original, fixed-date digest for this site. It does not claim to be a live news feed or a product of The Economist. Its dots navigate seven panels: a vertically scrolling seven-update digest, followed by six short reads. The same article image stays with its corresponding short read; different articles never share a source image.
